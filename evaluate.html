Evaluation kit

Watch first, then decide.

For a 20-minute session with a Tibetologist: what to give the participant, what the facilitator does, and where to record what happened.

Task sheetSession scriptFindings logSymbol review

Ground rules

  • Two or more sessions if possible, one with an experienced Tibetologist.
  • Observe tasks. Do not ask the participant to approve a feature list.
  • Ask permission before keeping any sample text. Nothing is collected automatically.
  • Separate what you saw from what you assume, and say what you would reject or defer.
  • This is a web prototype, not the macOS input method. Do not judge the installed experience from it.

Before: open the Writing Room in the participant's usual browser, check Tibetan displays, and ask for two or three difficult sequences from their field. After, within a day: complete the log, update the ranked backlog, and write down what to reject or defer.

Task sheet (for the participant)

Open the Writing Room. Type in EWTS; Tibetan appears. Highlighted text is still settling and becomes fixed when you press space or Enter. Think aloud if you can.

  1. Write a short phrase. Type a line of Tibetan you would normally write, such as a title or a sentence from your work. Tell us when it looks right.
  2. Correct a mistake. Change one syllable in the middle of the line. Then add one more word at the end.
  3. Sanskrit. Enter a Sanskrit mantra or a Sanskrit term from your own field, written in Tibetan script.
  4. Marks. Add a mark you often need that is not an ordinary letter: a head mark, a special shad, a bracket, a number, or something else. Use whatever route you like.
  5. Inspect and copy. Choose a sequence you find difficult. Look at what the page shows about it below the editor. Copy it.

Afterwards, if you are willing, open the symbol review page, mark any label or EWTS that is wrong, and add anything missing. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you press a button.

Session script (for the facilitator)

Opening, before the clock

  • Thank them. This tests the prototype, not the participant.
  • “Nothing you type is sent anywhere. I will ask before I keep any text.”
  • “If something is confusing, say so. That is the useful result.”
  • Ask consent to take notes, and separately to keep any sample text.

0–4 min: current workflow

Ask, and let them talk: what keyboard or input method do you use for Tibetan, and why? What makes you leave the keyboard? Which marks, Sanskrit stacks, names or terms are hard to enter? How often do you convert between Tibetan Unicode and EWTS? Does it differ for classical, modern, or manuscript transcription?

Observe: tools named, workarounds, pain points. Do not suggest features.

4–9 min: ordinary Tibetan in the Writing Room

Give tasks 1 and 2. Say nothing about how it works. Afterwards ask: “Was anything surprising?” “Did you check the output? How?”

Observe: speed, hesitation, errors, how they handle the highlighted text, whether they trust the result, editing friction (backspace, moving the cursor).

9–14 min: Sanskrit and rare punctuation

Give tasks 3 and 4. Let them find the Symbols panel themselves; if they have not after about a minute, note that, then point to it. Ask: “What is missing?” “Is the label what you would call it?”

Observe: do they search, browse by category, or type EWTS? Which marks do they reach for? Which do they look for and not find?

14–17 min: inspect and copy a difficult sequence

Give task 5 and point them to the inspector under the editor. Ask: “Would you want to see this while writing, or only on request?”

Observe: whether code points and the NFC line mean anything to them, whether the diagnostics help, whether they would copy this into their own document.

17–20 min: toolbar or on-demand menu

Describe both (no prototype yet): a small always-visible toolbar of marks and stacks beside the text, or a menu that appears only when asked, for example with a shortcut. Ask which they prefer and why. Then one question each, noting the answers:

  • Would completions help, or interrupt your work?
  • Which corrections would be unacceptable in a diplomatic transcription?
  • What would make you switch from your present method?

Closing

Ask if they are willing to mark entries on the symbol review page later. Ask permission to keep any sample text, and say what for. Thank them, and say what happens next and when they will hear back.
